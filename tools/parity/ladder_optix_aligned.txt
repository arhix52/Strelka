That file closes by saying this backend is noisier per sample than Metal, on the
evidence that `rel` ran about 1.3x the recorded column across every row. That
reading was wrong, and the way to tell is to stop measuring noise and bias in one
number. tools/parity/noise_check.py grades a render against a *converged* render
of the same backend and splits the residual by a 5x5 box: what a blur destroys is
per-pixel noise, what it keeps is bias.

Split that way, at 512 spp against a 4096-spp OptiX convergence:

    mean over 29 scenes     noise      bias
    OptiX  512 spp          0.0265    0.0001
    Metal  512 spp          0.0272    0.0018

0.98x. Twenty-five of the 29 rows agree to within 2%, which is the answer to the
question the old file asked: the two backends have the same per-sample variance,
and always did.


What the 1.3x actually was
--------------------------

A one-row vertical offset in the film position, which puts a full-contrast
residual on every silhouette in the frame and none anywhere else.

The measurement that isolates it. Grade both backends against the same Cycles
reference (`noise_check.py --ref cycles`), read the high-frequency column, and
restrict it to the three quarters of the pixels with no edge under them
(`--mask`): the two read **1.02x**, row by row, and mean noise 0.0208 against
0.0203. Drop the mask and the same comparison reads **2.36x**. Every bit of the
excess is in the quarter of the frame that has an edge in it -- which no amount of
sampling variance can do, because variance does not know where the edges are.

Fitting the offset between the two backends' renders confirms it directly. Each
row is the sub-pixel shift that minimises `rel`:

    scene                optix -> metal      metal -> cycles
    00_calibration       dy +0.91  dx +0.35  dy -0.46  dx +0.27
    02_basecolor         dy +0.98  dx +0.10  dy -0.21  dx +0.13
    20_mirror_and_floor  dy +1.00  dx +0.04  dy -0.04  dx -0.06
    24_orthographic      dy +1.02  dx -0.33  dy +0.11  dx +0.25
    28_hair              dy +1.04  dx -0.02  dy -0.19  dx +0.15

dy = +1.00 against Metal on every scene with an edge in it, and Metal aligned
with Cycles. Applying the fitted shift cut `rel` against Metal by 2-3x, which is
what says the residual was displacement and not disagreement.

The cause is one expression. Metal builds the film position as
`height - (y + jitter)`; OptiX flipped the integer first and added the jitter
after, `(height - y) + jitter`, which is the same band one row out -- and row 0
sampled `[height, height+1)`, off the film entirely. Fixed in generateCameraRay
(`OptixRender.cu`); the same expression feeds `screenSample`, so the motion
vectors were carrying the jitter with the wrong sign too.

After it, 27 of 29 rows reproduce the number Metal recorded to three digits in
both `rel` and `ratio`, and the vs-Metal column roughly halves.


The one row that was noise
--------------------------

`08_alpha_blend` was the only row still disagreeing, at 0.0287 noise against
Metal's 0.0183, and it is a sampler difference: the coverage draw came from a
standalone radical inverse rather than the Sobol table, on the argument that
adding a `SampleDimension` shifts every other draw in the renderer.

Being stratified across the samples of a pixel is not enough for a continuous
coverage test -- it has to be stratified *jointly* with where in the pixel the ray
went, and the Sobol index is scrambled per pixel, so pairing sample i's jitter
with the i-th radical inverse pairs a stratum with a shuffled one. `07_alpha_clip`
was never affected and says why: MASK resolves to 0 or 1, so the draw decides
nothing a neighbouring sample would decide differently.

With `eOpacity` added in the position Metal has it, the row reads 0.023 / 1.016 --
Metal's own number -- and noise 0.0161 against Metal's 0.0164. The dimension shift
cost nothing: every other row is unchanged to three digits.
